# Literature Review for Intent Based SDN SLA Enforcement

This review connects three verified studies to the repository's concrete design. The papers study broader and more general systems than this project; the comparison therefore distinguishes implemented mechanisms from concepts that remain outside the project scope.

## 1. Martini, Gharbaoui, and Castoldi (2023)

**Citation.** B. Martini, M. Gharbaoui, and P. Castoldi, “Intent-based network slicing for SDN vertical services with assurance: Context, design and preliminary experiments,” *Future Generation Computer Systems*, vol. 142, pp. 101–116, May 2023. DOI: [10.1016/j.future.2022.12.033](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.future.2022.12.033). The publisher record gives the title, venue, volume, pages, date, and DOI. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167739X2200437X)

**Problem and method.** The paper addresses the distance between a vertical service's business and connectivity goals and the technical descriptors required by NFV/SDN orchestration. Its intent layer accepts a declarative NILE specification, translates it into orchestration actions, deploys QoS-aware SDN-enabled slices through an ETSI NFV MANO platform, and continuously checks behavior against intent goals. The authors place slice creation, service chaining, connectivity, and assurance within the intent lifecycle.

**Evaluation and reported evidence.** The authors implement and evaluate the intent layer in two virtualized testbeds, focusing their reported scenario on a metro network. The evaluation asks whether the complete slice works under different conditions and measures instantiation, connectivity, and throughput; the article describes the results as preliminary feasibility/effectiveness evidence rather than a universal performance guarantee. The publisher's abstract and article record are the traceable sources for the architecture and evaluation scope. Specific values are not reproduced here where the publisher's accessible record does not expose them.

**Our understanding and relationship.** This work shows that an intent interface must be connected to actual service lifecycle and assurance mechanisms; a parser alone does not deliver a slice. Our repository follows the same broad intent-to-enforcement loop at a smaller scale: YAML schema and validation in `src/intent_manager/`, feasibility and route selection in `src/admission/` and `src/pathing/`, OpenFlow/HTB enforcement in `src/enforcement/`, and telemetry/control-loop handling in `src/telemetry/` and `src/control_loop` (implemented in the repository's enforcement/control-loop package). The project does not implement NILE, NFV MANO, VNFs, or multi-tenant service chaining.

**What this project adds and progress.** The repository makes bounded, reproducible software-level mechanisms explicit: multi-constraint path feasibility, residual capacity admission, competing-request preemption, versioned two-phase route updates, persistent audit events, and detector smoothing/hysteresis/dwell gates. Its B2/B3 fault scripts and raw run files exercise controlled closed-loop behavior on Mininet/OVS. These are demonstrable implementation details, not claims of novelty over the paper. B2 currently has ten runs, B3 one pilot; those counts do not establish a comparative result. The deterministic alternating trace added here exercises detector/dwell transitions only and is not live slice evaluation.

**Gaps.** Compared with this study, the project lacks NFV MANO integration, service descriptors/VNFs, NILE, multiple virtualized testbeds, and complete lifecycle or throughput evaluation. It should remain described as a compact SDN testbed implementation rather than an NFV slicing platform.

## 2. Mehmood, Kralevska, and Palma (2023)

**Citation.** K. Mehmood, K. Kralevska, and D. Palma, “Intent-driven autonomous network and service management in future cellular networks: A structured literature review,” *Computer Networks*, vol. 220, article 109477, Jan. 2023. DOI: [10.1016/j.comnet.2022.109477](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comnet.2022.109477). [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comnet.2022.109477)

**Problem and method.** This is a structured review, not a testbed implementation. It examines intent description and lifecycle, stakeholders, translation and orchestration, and how IBN interacts with SDN/NFV in future cellular networks. The authors report a search across five academic databases, retrieval of 5,484 records from 2015–2021, and a screened set of 60 studies. They synthesize a converged architecture and identify unresolved needs around context, intent representation, verification, resource feasibility, and learning.

**Evidence and findings.** The paper's method is literature search, filtering, and analytical synthesis; it has no single network topology, hardware platform, or measured SLA experiment. Its findings argue for domain-agnostic intent representation, lifecycle-aware processing, feedback/compliance verification, and further work on learning-enabled autonomy. These are the authors' review conclusions, not measurements from our repository.

**Our understanding and relationship.** The review gives a useful checklist for distinguishing “accepting an intent” from translating, checking feasibility, enforcing, observing, and assuring it. The repository implements a deliberately constrained subset: a fixed YAML schema, explicit feasibility, deterministic route computation, instrumentation, a closed loop, and a dashboard/audit view. It does not implement natural-language intent input, ML/NLP translation, multi-domain orchestration, or learning.

**What this project adds and progress.** In contrast to a review, this repository contains executable components and tests for one bounded SDN topology. Tests cover intent validation, path feasibility, residual capacity, controller enforcement, measurement handling, dwell suppression, and causal audit. The live controlled B2 experiments provide measured event and path-install timings for this testbed. The contribution is an implementation exercise and traceable evaluation artifact, not a new general IBN architecture.

**Gaps.** The repository does not address the breadth or cross-domain semantic issues catalogued by the survey, nor does its current B2/B3 evidence validate intent generality. The missing original E1–E8 metric contract also prevents claiming alignment with the original project evaluation plan.

## 3. Hermosilla, Martinez-Julia, Lopez, and Skarmeta (2025)

**Citation.** A. Hermosilla, P. Martinez-Julia, D. R. Lopez, and A. F. Skarmeta, “Multi-Domain Network Intent Policy Enforcement,” *Computer Modeling in Engineering & Sciences*, vol. 145, no. 3, pp. 4279–4316, 2025. DOI: [10.32604/cmes.2025.072607](https://doi.org/10.32604/cmes.2025.072607). [Publisher full text](https://www.techscience.com/CMES/v145n3/64980/html)

**Problem and method.** The study asks how to improve the correctness of enforcement when intents span multiple domains. It proposes a model mapping design descriptors to policy-enforcement accuracy, uses that model to guide a multi-domain enforcement system, and includes intent translation, policy generation, policy verification, closed control loops, multi-agent resolution, and platform orchestration. The reported implementation uses Python and Hy, SWI-Prolog via Janus, Dockerized components, an NFV/OpenStack environment, and inter-domain VPN/control-plane connections. Its semantic path includes policy expressions in SWRL/OCL-related forms and policy verification against observed variables.

**Evaluation and findings.** The authors report experimentation on a large-scale multi-domain platform across sites separated by more than 10,000 km. They report 10% greater accuracy, up to 99% overall enforcement accuracy, enforcement below 1.75 seconds even for their most complex intents, and model predictions up to 99% accurate relative to observed behavior. These figures apply to that paper's system, descriptors, and experiment, not to this project. The publisher article gives the publication date (issue published 23 Dec. 2025) and evaluation claims.

**Our understanding and relationship.** The central lesson is that policy translation and execution need explicit verification and multiple feedback loops; an accepted command is not evidence that the network meets the intent. This repository does a narrower form of enforcement checking: it validates intent structure, verifies switch rule installation and route changes in live scenario scripts, persists detector events and causal audit records, and exposes observed measurements through the dashboard. It does not semantically decompose policies, verify arbitrary policy logic, federate domain agents, or coordinate remote domains.

**What this project adds and progress.** The SDN checkpoint includes a small end-to-end demonstration from structured intent through route installation to detector-triggered rerouting, with strict two-phase updates and 10-run B2 fault data. The B3 strict/damped profile has a single recorded pilot. This work is complementary in scale and evidence type; it does not improve the paper's accuracy result or claim multi-domain performance.

**Gaps.** The project lacks multi-domain resolution, semantic policy verification, a formal accuracy model, OpenStack service orchestration, and geographically distributed experiments. A future extension could add structured assertions that compare all declared guarantees with telemetry and route state, then test them across independently managed domains.

## Synthesis and comparison

The papers cover complementary levels. Martini et al. connect declarative intents to NFV/SDN slice lifecycle and service assurance. Mehmood et al. map the wider intent lifecycle and highlight representation, feasibility, and feedback as open design needs. Hermosilla et al. focus on policy correctness and multi-domain enforcement, including explicit verification and measured enforcement accuracy. Together, they show why a credible IBN prototype must link intent semantics, resource checks, enforcement, and feedback while stating its scope.

This repository implements a constrained, local SDN version of that chain. It reuses the general intent-driven/closed-loop ideas, adapts them into a fixed YAML grammar and Mininet/OVS topology, and adds concrete reference mechanisms such as residual-capacity admission, preemption, two-phase updates, detector dwell, and audit. The supplied evidence supports functionality and a B2 pilot-series measurement. It does not support claims of new algorithms, production-grade 5G SLA guarantees, improvement over the three papers, or a full statistically supported B2/B3 comparison. The original E1–E8 definitions and ST-13/ST-14 briefs are absent; the separately stored protocol proposal is not an approved contract.
